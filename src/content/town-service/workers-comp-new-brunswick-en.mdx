## What it costs in New Brunswick, in ranges

The numbers below are statewide reference ranges. They are useful for knowing
whether a price you were given is normal, not for predicting yours.

In NJ, workers' comp premium is calculated per $100 of payroll, multiplied by
the NJCRIB class code rate and your experience mod. Published rates for
construction trades run far above clerical work, so the same wage costs very
differently by trade.

There is no list price. The final number depends on concrete factors:

- **The class code.** This is the biggest factor: the same wage costs very differently by trade.
- **Actual payroll.** Premium is calculated per $100 of payroll and audited at year end.
- **The experience mod.** Your claims history adjusts the rate up or down.
- **Whether you use subcontractors.** Subs without their own coverage usually get added to your auditable payroll.
- **The kind of work within the trade.** Work at height or with heavy machinery does not classify like ground-level work.

That is what moves the number. What does not move is whether you are required to
carry it.
