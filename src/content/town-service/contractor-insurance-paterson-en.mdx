## What contracting in Paterson actually looks like

Paterson runs on small crews. Two or three people, one truck, tools loaded and
unloaded every day, and work that is almost never on the block where you live.
Most contractors coming out of 07501, 07503 or 07513 finish the week somewhere in
Bergen County: Ridgewood, Wyckoff, Fair Lawn, Glen Rock. Bigger houses, clients
with lawyers, and general contractors who work off written contracts.

That is where the problem we hear about most on WhatsApp shows up. It is not
landing the job. It is that the general contractor wants the **certificate of
insurance (COI)** on Thursday so you can start Monday, and the policy you bought
over the phone eight months ago does not say what the contract requires.
Sometimes the limit is $500,000 and the contract asks for $1,000,000. Sometimes
the limit is fine but there is no *additional insured* endorsement naming the
general. Sometimes the policy was written as *handyman* work and you are doing
structural work.

Any one of those three stops the job. And stopping it Thursday afternoon, with a
crew already committed for Monday, is a lost week that usually does not come back.

The other classic Paterson situation is the contractor who has worked well for
years, no claims, and never formalized anything because the work always came by
word of mouth. That holds up until you want to move up. Once you go from
remodeling kitchens for neighbors to taking contracts from a general, insurance
stops being optional and becomes the price of admission.

## What it costs, in ranges

A New Jersey contractor usually ends up with two separate policies. **General
liability (GL)** responds to damage you cause to a third party or their property.
**Workers' compensation** covers your people if they get hurt on the job. They are
quoted separately and paid separately.

Published ranges for a small crew run roughly **$900 to $3,000 a year** for
general liability. Workers' comp has no comparable range, because it is not
quoted off a price list — it is calculated on your actual payroll.

That GL range is wide on purpose, because price moves with concrete factors:

- **The trade.** Roofing and demolition price differently than painting or finish
  carpentry. Working at height changes the risk profile.
- **Payroll.** NJ workers' comp is calculated per $100 of payroll, multiplied by
  the state's class code rate.
- **Years in business and claims history.** A contractor with five clean years
  does not price like one who opened last month.
- **The limits you carry.** Going from $500,000 to $1,000,000 does not double the
  cost, but it does move the number.

One thing worth being clear about from the start: **general liability does not
cover your tools.** GL responds to damage you cause to a third party or their
property. Your tools fall under a separate coverage the industry calls *tools and
equipment* or *inland marine*. In a city where the truck spends the night parked
on the street, that distinction matters more than it sounds.

Now, part of those limits is not your decision. There is a floor the state sets
and another the client sets, and they are not the same number.

Note the renewal date: **March 31.** Every year in Paterson we see contractors
find out in April that their registration lapsed, usually because a client went
and checked. A lapsed registration is not just paperwork: without it, a home
improvement contract can be called into question.

On the client side, the working standard in Bergen County and on commercial jobs
in Passaic County sits above the state minimum. A $1,000,000 per-occurrence limit
appears in most contracts we see, along with additional insured status and
sometimes a waiver of subrogation. The law does not require that; the contract
does. Meeting the state minimum does not mean you qualify for the job.
